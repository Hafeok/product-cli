# C0 — the control corpus: selection procedure and constraint

**Status:** `[PROPOSED]` — session output, not ratified.
**Dated:** 2026-08-26. **Gate:** 2b §3.2. **C-O-9, promoted to a C0 completion condition.**

Mechanism 1 is weakened by the provenance audit; mechanism 2 does not exist yet; so mechanism 3
carries the factoring. Both existing corpora were chosen by the factorer after reading the slots. The
control is what stops mechanism 3 being a self-assessment.

---

## 1. The procedure matters more than the corpus

**The requirement, restated exactly:** "chosen by someone other than whoever factored". That is the
entire content of the control. A procedure that routes back through the factorer produces a corpus
that may be excellent and is not a control.

### 1.1 What is not available, stated first

**No chooser outside the programme is available in this setup.** Not "hard to arrange" — not
available. The session cannot recruit one, and there is no third party in the loop. Any procedure
proposed below is a control against **factorer** shaping, which is what C-O-9 asks for, and **not**
a control against **programme** shaping. The principal is the PRD's author and the schema's author;
a corpus he selects is independent of the factorer and is not independent of the programme.

That distinction should be recorded wherever the control's result is cited, because a control
described without it will be read as stronger than it is.

### 1.2 Options considered and rejected

| Option | Rejected because |
|---|---|
| The session picks a third corpus | The factorer choosing its own control. Not a control. |
| The session spawns a subagent with a brief | The brief routes through the factorer. Controls against corpus taste, not against the constraint set — which is where the shaping would live. Worse: it *looks* independent, which makes it more dangerous than no control. |
| The session picks by a rule it invents | Same defect, one level up. The rule is the choice. |

### 1.3 `[PROPOSED]` The procedure

**Pre-registered selection rule, authored by the principal, executed by the session blind.**

1. **The principal fixes a selection rule** that determines a corpus without further discretion —
   naming a source of candidates and a deterministic position in it, not a shortlist to choose from.
2. **The rule is committed before it is executed**, on this branch, in this directory.
3. **The session executes it and takes what it yields**, including if the result is awkward, thin, or
   unflattering to the factoring.
4. **No re-roll.** A second execution after an unwelcome first result makes the rule a shortlist and
   the control a selection. If the first result is genuinely unusable, that fact is recorded against
   the *rule* and the rule is replaced by the principal — visibly, as a superseding act, never as a
   quiet retry.

**Why pre-registration and not merely delegation.** Delegation alone still leaves discretion at
selection time; a rule committed before execution removes it. It is the same move as the falsifier
discipline this programme already runs on: state the observation that would kill it *before* looking.

**The residue, stated rather than engineered away.** The session drafted this procedure, so the
procedure is factorer-authored even though the choice is not. What escapes entirely is only the
principal authoring the rule himself. That is the recommendation.

### 1.4 If the principal declines

Then **there is no control**, and C0 completes with the control recorded as **absent**, not as
satisfied by a weaker substitute. A control chosen by the factorer, or by a proxy briefed by the
factorer, should not be dressed as one — and a C0 that honestly records "no control was available"
is more useful later than a C0 that records a control which was not one.

## 2. The constraint: not governance-shaped

The residual risk named at Gate 2 is that "unlike statute" has been satisfied by **vocabulary**
rather than by **structure**. Bandmaster and Steward both have a body that settles something and a
party that must then act accordingly. Strip the legal words and the shape is still: authority →
compliance.

**`[PROPOSED]` constraint set for the control corpus:**

| # | Constraint | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | **No party is accountable to another for conformance.** Nobody audits, sanctions, certifies or withholds anything. | This is the operative one. It is what "not governance-shaped" means. |
| C2 | A decision is nonetheless made, and behaviour is expected to follow from it. | Otherwise nothing is exercised. |
| C3 | The decision and the behaviour are separated by at least two transcription steps. | The chain must have links. |
| C4 | Interpretation is genuinely contested in the domain, by the domain's own lights. | Exercises interpretation and residue non-trivially. |
| C5 | Not statute. Not C#. Not this programme's own subject matter. | The standing rules, plus: a corpus about decision governance would be maximally self-shaping. |
| C6 | Pre-existing, and not authored for this purpose. | A corpus written to fit is the circularity again. |

C1 is the constraint that would have excluded both existing corpora, which is the point of adding it.

## 3. An illustration, and what naming it costs

The constraint class is **non-empty**, and demonstrating that is worth something — a constraint set
nothing satisfies is a way of concluding that the control is impossible without saying so.

A domain that meets C1–C6: **a musical score and its performance.** A composer's marking is a
decision; what a player does is the behaviour; autograph, published edition and performing edition
are distinct embodiments of differing authority; interpretation is the domain's own word for the act;
residue is enormous and the domain says so itself. And **nobody is accountable to anybody** — a
performer who reads a marking differently is not in breach of anything.

**Naming it disqualifies it.** The factorer has now named it, so selecting it would be factorer
choice wearing a procedure. It is offered as proof that C1–C6 are satisfiable, at the cost of one
candidate, and it should be **excluded by name** from whatever selection rule the principal fixes.

That trade is stated so it can be rejected: if the principal would rather have had the candidate than
the demonstration, this section was a mistake and the candidate is spent either way.

## 4. What the control does and does not test

**Does:** whether the factoring's elements can be exercised by a corpus the factorer did not choose
and did not shape the constraints for — and specifically whether elements that survived on Bandmaster
and Steward survive on something with no authority relation in it at all.

**Does not:** rescue mechanism 1. The provenance audit's result stands whatever the control corpus
shows. An element that entered post-corpus entered post-corpus; a third corpus can show it is
*exercisable*, never that it was *derived from the schema*.

Those are different claims and the C0 record should not let the second borrow credit from the first.
